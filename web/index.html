    Chord practice
    Solo practice
    How this works
    • Click a bar to work on it. Everything you play is checked against that bar - it is the one named beside Expecting. Arrow keys move along the chart.
    • Click the same bar again for the substitutions you could put in its place. Choosing one rewrites the bar.
    • The keys below stay down. Click them one at a time to build a chord - they are a voicing you are holding, not a piano you are playing. Click one again, or Clear keys, to let go.
    • Show me one puts a voicing that works under your hands. Press it again for the next one, and again after that.
    • A MIDI keyboard is better than clicking. Connect one under Practice.
    • Sustain holds what you have played after the keys lift, and a real pedal on a connected keyboard does the same.
    • Solo, at the top, asks a different question of the same notes: not whether a voicing fits the bar, but where each note you play sits against it. The page turns its light down when you go, so you always know which one you are in.
    • Click a bar to solo over it. Every note you play is read against that bar's chord - a chord tone, a scale tone, an approach note or outside - and the key lights in that colour as it sounds. Arrow keys move along the chart.
    • A note outside the harmony is not called a mistake when you play it. It is open: you have started something and nothing yet knows how it ends. What the dock says is the thing that is true at that instant - which note a step away would land it, and what that note is in the chord.
    • Then the next two notes decide it. Step home from it, or take the target from both sides first, and it is read again as an approach note that counts as landing. Go somewhere else and it settles as outside, and the dock says so then - two notes later, which is the earliest it could honestly be said. Nothing is graded on a note while it is still open.
    • Click the same bar again for the scales that fit it. Picking one is the point: the engine stops holding you to its own first answer and holds you to the scale you chose instead.
    • Start a take to make it keep count. It runs until you stop it, across as many bars as you walk through, and stopping freezes the summary rather than clearing it.
    • Every bar keeps its own score. A bar you have played over carries a strip in those same three colours and a percentage for how it went, so the bar that got away is one you can see down the chart rather than one you have to read about. It is a reading of the bar, not a mark: nothing here calls a note wrong.
    • The keys do not stay down here. A line is played, not held: each note sounds and lets go, which is the opposite of chord practice and the same keyboard.
    • Which scale? names the scale this bar is being read against and puts it on the keyboard, so the target is in front of you before a note is played.
    • A MIDI keyboard is better than clicking. Connect one under Practice.
    • Chords, at the top, asks the other question of the same notes: not where a note sits against the bar, but whether a whole voicing says what the symbol says.

    The Practice menu decides which shape you are working on - shell, rootless, two-handed - and everything you play is judged against that.

    The Practice menu decides the vocabulary you are working out of - the modes, melodic minor, bebop, the pentatonics - and every bar is read against a scale from it.

    Not built yet
    Playing in time

    Solo practice is static on purpose for now. The bar you are on is the bar you chose, it stays there until you move, and nothing anywhere is counting time.

    • In time would put a clock behind the chart: a tempo, a count-in, and the bar moving on its own underneath you while you play over it.
    • Rhythm would start to count. With no clock, where in the bar a note landed is invisible from here - which is why an avoid note is named rather than marked down. Sitting on one and passing through it look identical until something knows what the beat was.
    • It is the same feature as a metronome and a practice loop, so it should arrive with them rather than ahead of them.

    The engine is already shaped for it: a take has no clock in it, and the bar it reads against is set from outside. Driving that from a tempo is the part that is missing - nothing already built would have to change.
